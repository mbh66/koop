## What was discussed

### Why we are meeting

William McDillon opened the meeting. Those present had signed a commitment at the training [MS]. Rotary invested in the training, so the group was asked to commit to the work or to step back; nobody is forced to take part. Keith Radmore, who chairs the Greyton Rotary Club [MS], was introduced.

### One brand for many businesses

Michael Haupt explained what a cooperative could do. Two valley brands, a coffee brand and a chocolate maker in Greyton, were given as examples that people recognise at a glance because their look and story are the same everywhere [MS]. Points made:

- Each business can build its own brand alone, or the group can build one brand with many businesses inside it. One brand with the valley's heritage behind it tells a stronger story and can charge more.
- Deciding together is harder than deciding alone. The group must agree on a logo, colours and a story.
- One person selling alone usually sells to one customer at a time. Together, the group could supply bigger buyers such as restaurants in Greyton, which now bring fresh produce from Cape Town every day [MS]. Bigger buyers need a steady supply, so the group would need surplus and planting across the seasons.
- Every product under the brand must meet the same standard. One product that falls short harms the whole brand.
- The cooperative should own the trademark, website and social media accounts as its assets, so nobody can leave with them.
- The cooperative would serve three kinds of buyer. 
	- Visitors want the story and the experience as much as the product: labels, demonstrations, places to learn how things are made. 
	- Shops and restaurants want the same product at the same price for months ahead. 
	- Friends and family want fair prices and healthy food.
- The group cannot compete with big brands on price, so its products must be special enough that people are proud to choose them.
- In time, the cooperative could open its own outlets, including a farm stall on the N2 and small shops in each village, so more money stays in the valley. Spaza shops mostly sell goods that result in money leaving the valley [MS].
- Once its products are consistent, the cooperative could also offer services and bring in other businesses under its banner if they meet its standards.
- A cooperative gives each member one vote, whatever land, money or knowledge they bring [MS].
- Mondragon in Spain was given as an example of a large cooperative that started with a small group [MS].

Product ideas raised included frozen vegetables from heirloom or organic seed, baby food, skin products, and food with accommodation.

### Security of gardens

Theft and damage in gardens were raised as a risk to the group's work. The group suggested inviting the chairperson of the Community Police Forum and looking at neighbourhood watches. It was also said that theft and sabotage come from a system that has failed people, so security measures alone will not solve them.

### Values and story

Cooperatives often fail when members do not first agree on their values, so the group was asked to get to know each other and agree on shared values [MS]. The group also discussed the story its brand would tell. One proposal: "we started small, and now we are sustainable". The view was that values and story belong in the group's strategic plan, and should be worked out there.

Someone asked how indigenous knowledge in products would be protected. The United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples was named as support for this [MS]. How it applies here is still to be checked.

### Support from Rotary and others

- Rotary cannot register a cooperative for the group [MS]. Experts will come and recommend what suits the group [MS].
- A potential funder is coming to Theewaterskloof [MS]. Keith Radmore will tell her about the group, and the Rotary president will also go [MS].
- The group will show its garden in Voorstekraal on 18 November 2026 at 14:30 [MS].
- Michael Haupt offered to arrange a visit to Babylonstoren for those who take this on, and to ask for an invitation to its yearly suppliers' meeting. Babylonstoren makes gift baskets with produce from the valley and is looking for more products that meet its standard [MS].
- The group wants Theewaterskloof Municipality to attend the next meeting, give a date for a meeting about forming the cooperative, and bring the Small Enterprise Development and Finance Agency (SEDFA) and the Department of Agriculture to explain how to start one.

### Youth, sport and tourism

A youth development programme was described, run as a business that anyone can join [MS]. It aims to bring sport back to the valley: table tennis, soccer, tennis and canoeing on the river, and to fix up sports fields [MS]. The valley has no guides for visitors, and plans were raised for eco-tourism: hiking routes, mountain guides and B&Bs.

### Soup kitchens and food security

- Donations for children are made each year on 29 November through a soup kitchen [MS].
- Soup kitchens in the valley buy their food each month, some of it from outside the valley [MS]. The group could grow food for them.
- Funders want reports on what their donations achieve [MS].
- Soup kitchens need a structure first, under a plan by sector (agriculture, welfare, sport), with leaders who organise how food reaches the community.

### Questions and ideas from the group

Each person was asked for one question or idea:

1. A venue to process and make products, with storage.
2. Paintings and billboards for the cooperative.
3. T-shirts with the cooperative's name, and sports development in the community.
4. Food security for Genadendal, using our own resources. Register as soon as possible, because time is key. Come together more, create jobs and bring more business into the community.
5. Set up the cooperative's structure now, without waiting for the municipality, so the vision can start.
6. The logo, products and services must each carry a story.
7. Markets and shops that sell local butchers' products.
8. Food security through garden development and soup kitchens.
9. Bring products to the next meeting, with how each is made, and plan for tourists visiting the community.
10. Each person writes their own idea on paper. Launch the cooperative here.

### Anything else

- Eggs in Genadendal were noted for discussion.
- A night market in Genadendal was suggested.
- Bring more people and businesses into the group.
- A lunch was suggested, with children invited for painting, art and craft, and sport.
- Some wanted the meeting to focus on deciding what happens next, and to hold other questions for later. A separate meeting about forming the cooperative will be held once a date is set.
